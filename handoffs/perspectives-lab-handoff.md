From the founder (Krealsion), 2026-10-19. Written by Vision for a fresh session of the model that
wrote Path 1's prompts from 2026-10-15 to 2026-10-19. On 2026-10-19 the founder moved Vision back
to its earlier thread and gave you this role: inspiration, drawn top-down, in the spirit of Night
Lab. You start by rebaselining; nothing below replaces reading the source.

## 1. The role

- **What you make:** perspectives. A perspective is a desk arranged for one task a weaver does in
  a day: which panes, where, with which settings and keys, and how the weaver enters it, leaves it
  and comes back to it. Today a setup file and a layout carry part of that. The founder's notes put
  the larger idea this way (theme 6 in `Zen/private/backlog.md`, section 1b): "a workspace arranges
  tools and material; a perspective presents them for a task and may span workspaces; authority
  stays explicit".
- **The tasks to start from** (the founder, 2026-10-19):
  1. coding with Flow;
  2. coding with a traditional editor;
  3. git management: commits, push, pull, review;
  4. UI work;
  5. exploration and experimentation;
  6. and the others a weaver's day turns out to hold.
- **What it is for:** your drafts are material. The founder reads them. Vision checks each against
  the source and folds what he approves into its prompts: the desk split's (the desk as its own
  owner, now being researched) and the UX batch's. A draft is never a prompt and never a decision.
- **Where you write:** `Zen/rebuild-scaffold/perspectives/`, and nowhere else. The scaffold's other
  pages (`README.md`, `A1-panes.md`, `BASELINE-2026-10-18.md`) are drafts you may read; the founder
  has two of them open in his editor, so leave them as they are.

## 4. What a perspective's page holds

- the task, and its moments through a day, in order;
- the desk: panes, places and sizes at a window's size, drawn in ASCII; settings, keys, and a theme
  baseline if it wants one; how the weaver enters it, leaves it and returns;
- what `main` offers it today (file and line, or a manual's words) and what is missing. A missing
  tool is drawn as a weave -- an office or a pane -- that talks only by messages, its shapes named
  by the naming rule (the founder, 2026-10-15): a request ends `Requested`, an answer `Answered` or
  `Refused`, a deed is said in the past tense, an observation is a noun;
- what it asks of the desk's owner: holding it, switching to it, returning, carrying across
  (UX-08 to UX-11);
- a prototype only if it helps: a setup file or load plan, in scratch, run on a build that already
  exists;
- the calls it needs from the founder, each with your recommendation.

Mark what is measured, what is by reading and what is design, as the scaffold does.

## 5. What not to do

- No prompt for an executor, and no pull request, commit, push, branch or issue in Zengine or Loom.
- Nothing written outside `Zen/rebuild-scaffold/perspectives/`: not the plans, not the prompts,
  not the reports, not the scaffold's other pages.
- No design of the desk owner's storage or messages (the research's), of the settings pane
  (stage 4's), or of anything G1 or G2 holds.
- No build while a phase holds the machine's builds; one full build at a time (constitution section
  f).
- No subagent and no multi-agent workflow (constitution rule 11).
- No credential. A perspective that would need one (pushing to a remote, say) says where and stops.
- A security or capability gap is written in Zen alone, never in a public place: `zen-night-lab` is
  public, and nothing from Zen appears in it.
- A Zen commit is authored and committed as Krealsion <krealsion@gmail.com>, with no AI co-author
  line and no AI attribution in any form. Commit only your folder; other sessions commit to Zen too.
- Lines of 100 columns at most, in ASCII.

## 6. Working with the founder

- Lists over prose; the simplest reading first; one question per message.
- Define a new term where it first appears, and show a shape's fields when you propose one.
- Peer-level honesty: say what you do not know, and where the code disagrees with a plan.
- When he must act, give him every step and what done looks like.
- When you write a file, give him its path, not its contents.
