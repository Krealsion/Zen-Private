## 1. Working with Joshua — what this chat learned

- **One thing per message.** During a review, one decision at a time: your read in a few lines,
  then a suggested one-sentence answer, then "yes, no, or something else?" He will say "rapid
  fire" when he wants all of them in one message; keep the same shape, chained.
- **Lists.** He reads lists; he does not read dense prose while thinking.
- **Questions being begged: ask whether he wants them answered.** Do not answer questions he did
  not know to ask yet. The failure this chat had: two questions asked, two answered, then three
  paste blocks and a schedule he had not asked for.
- **"Before the merge" first.** When reviewing an RB, lead with anything that should change before
  he merges; everything else is accept.
- **Complete instructions, no play-by-play.** He does not read report-backs word for word. An
  instruction carries its own context: exact command, exact file, what "done" looks like.
- **Insert document changes yourself.** The Filesystem connector is allowed on
  `G:\programming\cpp\Zen`. Small edits (a machine-file line, a prompt's addendum) go in directly
  by `edit_file`; show the diff; he checks. Tested twice this chat; the tool kept CRLF.
- **The split of labour.** His: decisions, merging PRs, repository settings. Ceecee's: everything
  else, including housekeeping. Ceecee has `gh` and opens PRs; he merges. He does not use
  worktrees (two `claude/*` worktrees exist in `Zen/`, untouched, harmless).
- **Measure before deciding.** Both repositories are public (`Krealsion/Zengine`,
  `Krealsion/Loom`); cloning them into the sandbox and reading the code beat inferring from the
  documents. The split's unit was decided on a section-by-register matrix and a COMDAT census,
  not on the hand-off's summary.

## 4. Debts and pressures carried (from the three RBs)

1. **Four routed documents over budget** — `operators`, `panes`, `realization`, `surface` — each a
   register phase in the shape now proven twice (Workshop laws, verification methods).
2. **85 methods applied nowhere**: 48 are the two mutation registers whole, 21 of 24 witness rows.
   The harness programs (`mutate.sh`, `bmpdiff.py`, the P/Invoke class, the canary harness) live
   with phase records. A `tools/mutate/` or `tools/witness/` home is the seam; the trigger is the
   next live-witness or mutation phase — Phase 2 is that.
3. **Rule s has no predicate.** Three wrong `Struct::member` spellings came from the sweep's sheet
   choosing a struct by name; rule n now refuses the result, nothing refuses the cause.
4. **Rule n's scope tracker fails open one way:** a `{` hidden in a raw string or block comment
   leaves a struct unpopped. Zero occurrences today; a masked scan like `cxx.py`'s would close it.
5. **Two entry forms, one checker (~98 KB, ~3 s Windows / ~5 s Linux):** a third register family
   (the seam laws in `panes.md`, say) wants a table of forms or a split into predicates and
   walkers. The register wrap helper is still a scratchpad script; `tools/` wants it.
6. **The split's tool is history's:** a rerun from `475fc96` would undo the residues; its header
   does not say so. `weave_run.cpp` is the odd file (`finish_draft_first` stranded by constants; a
   header reorder fixes it, not the applier).
7. **Header-parse cost** is the next measurable thing about build time, not urgent.
8. **Windows-filesystem debts from before this arc:** WL-FILES-04 UNWITNESSED on libstdc++ and
   Windows, `admit_path` on MSVC, the tests' other `remove_all`s. These are what a local MinGW
   lane is for; it exists now.
9. **The router does not state the split-area rule** (ids permanent, files hold an area's ids
   non-contiguously); `session.md` / `session-restore.md` is the precedent.
10. **The phase-tag predicate keys on a list of families** (WL, VM, TIMER, POP, KERN); a new
    family's parenthetical citation reads as a tag until the list gains it — a visible red.

## 5. Lessons for the prompts you write

- **A proof rule needs an exception for sentences the change makes false.** The residues' "byte-
  identical outside the moved entries" rule forbade Ceecee from fixing `session.md`'s preamble the
  moment the seam made it false; it took a pre-merge commit.
- **Name the budget.** "BECAUSE lines are not trimmed to fit a budget" was read as the register
  byte budget; 66 reasons lost their measurement to a line-count rule. Say which budget.
- **One phase with a sheet is safe.** Sheet → applier → proof regenerated from START means a wrong
  plan is a rerun, not a redo; a single conditional STOP (register over budget) was enough.
- **A prediction is a measurement's job, not a gate's.** The split's headline (2.40 assembles)
  was predicted from a Linux census and measured at END on the real toolchain; the census landed
  within 1% of MinGW's number.
