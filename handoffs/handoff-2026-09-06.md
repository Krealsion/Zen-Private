## 1. Working with Joshua — what this chat learned, on top of the 09-04 hand-off's §1

- **"Simplicity is the ultimate sophistication."** His standing rule for every response. When he
  doesn't understand, give the simplest interpretation first. Don't enumerate permutations of what
  is possible — only that none of them has an issue. A reply that lands as a grab bag of
  relevant-sounding information fails him; he wants a collaborator who knows how to explain.
- **Loom is held to a much higher standard than anything above it.** He is "only significantly
  concerned about Loom changes." Zengine is taken seriously, not to the same standard; the layers
  above Loom are replaceable and useful. Any Loom change, even one documentation paragraph, gets
  read whole and checked against source before you say "merge."
- **Ceremony** is his word for the number and complexity of maker actions — interaction and
  authority decisions — to land one intent. Not time. Protocol latency is "protocol cost."
- **Before the merge first.** Read the PR's branch, not the report's account of it. Twice this
  chat a branch had something the RB described accurately and that still needed changing before
  `main` (an authority hole; a false framing in package prose). He merges on shared approval.
- **The review gate as it now runs:** he brings the RB's Decisions asked; you give the read per
  decision in a few lines, a suggested one-sentence answer, then "yes, no, or something else?" He
  says "rapid fire" for the rest; he has done so every time the remaining decisions were the same
  kind. The approval is appended to the prompt file and Ceecee re-runs the same `run …` line.
- **Path only.** When you write a file to his machine, give the path. Never echo its contents.
- **Ask whether he wants a begged question answered.** He will ask the real question himself
  ("who is compiling the code that calls the maker method?") and it will be the right one.
- **He apologized for the poisoned hand-off and asked not to be held to it.** He wasn't; the pressure
  made the documents better. He says *good job* when he means it and it is true.

## 5. Lessons for the prompts you write

- **Put the FCO-v2 §1–§4 in READ.** Every prompt this chat wrote after the rewrite did; Ceecee's
  framings stopped drifting.
- **State the tree's facts in WHY with `file:line` and say "verify, do not inherit."** Ceecee
  found and recorded contradictions twice; that is the mechanism working.
- **A witness that widens authority is the wrong witness.** A prompt line ("state read through
  `zen.PokeRead`") made a plan teach Workshop to poke any weave. The oven painting its own state
  read the same fact with no grant added. Read the plan for authority creep your own words caused.
- **Installation truth is never written by a maker gesture.** The shipped catalog and the shipped
  plan beside the executable are the installation; a maker's rows go to the project root, and one
  launch rule finds them.
- **Two PRs in order when a tooling change and a feature share a phase.** The checker change landed
  alone first; the package landed on a checker that already knew its family. Same again for reload
  (stage a) and authoring (stage b).
- **The runner is a second machine.** A staged non-image hung `LoadLibrary` behind a modal on the
  runner's Windows and cost five hours; the desktop never showed it. When CI reds and the desktop
  doesn't, trust the runner.
- **A quick-repair prompt can carry three commits and two repositories** if each commit is one
  thing with its own proof and the Loom fence lifts for exactly what the founder named.
