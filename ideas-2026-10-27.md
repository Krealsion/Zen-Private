# The founder's ideas, 2026-10-27

**Source:** the founder's list after the feel walk (2026-10-27), held back while the week's work
moved; "not the most organized", and he does not expect all of it, or most. Sorted here by where
each lands, each with a rough size (S: hours; M: about a day of a lane; L: days) and what it meets
already: a UX-nn is a draft in [ux-batch.md](ux-batch.md) (2026-10-14) asking the same, walk-n a
finding of the feel walk ([report](../reportbacks/start-here-feel-walk-RB.md)), #n a Zengine issue.
The list as he wrote it closes this page.

**Nothing here is started.** The UX batch's drafts were to be filed after the manuals and the desk
split (backlog section 1b); both are done, so the batch, with these folded in, can be filed.

## 1. The desk and its panes

- **A pane's own close and move buttons** at its top right, each one a pane may switch off in its
  defaults. M. Meets the `= ?` already there.
- **A pane shown appears centered, in arrange mode**; a Workshop setting puts it at the mouse
  instead. M. The founder's answer to walk-1b (2026-10-27): wherever a pane opens by itself it may
  overlap -- the weaver's own arranging, a small screen -- so the weaver places it. Taken with
  two of Vision's refinements: arrange mode only the first time a pane opens in a setup, then
  where it was left; only for an open that brings the keys, never a seat without them. Walk-1a
  (a view already on the desk jumping in front) is a fix of its own.
- **Arrange mode is a state no one can miss** (the founder, 2026-10-27, after a friend's try:
  clicking outside the pane being arranged ended it, and nothing said why): every other pane's
  colours fade and the one being moved stays as it is; the words "Arranging Mode Active", and
  beneath them, smaller, the pane's name ("Powers") or "all panes" when the whole desk is
  being arranged. M, in both windows.
- **Where a pane was is kept per setup across closing and opening**; "remove from the setup" takes
  the place of today's "hide pane", and a small x hides it, keeping its place. M. Meets UX-04.
- **Arrange markers on a box of their own**, not over the `?`. S.
- **A box around each icon at a pane's top right** (the founder, 2026-10-27), so a weaver is sure
  which one the pointer is on. S to M. For the roadmap, with the pane's own buttons above.
- **The palette opens centered** (the founder, 2026-10-27), whether or not arrange mode follows.
  S. In the desk round ([prompt](../prompts/desk-room.md)).
- **A manual opens over its subject**, 8 to 16 px of that pane showing on every edge (the founder,
  2026-10-27). S to M. Meets UX-01. In the desk round. Later: a manual closes when it is no
  longer chosen, unless a pin among its top right icons keeps it.
- **Accordions as docks**: a slot holding one pane and sized to it, on one of the four edges,
  anchored along that edge; two-sided, switching the panes of one area by opening the one chosen; a
  setting for the bar's length. L.
- **The Pane Manager sorted alphabetically**, and its wheel scrolling the hidden rows before it
  moves the cursor. S each.
- **A row cut at a pane's foot counts as cut**, so the pane scrolls to show it whole. S.
- **Leaving with unsaved work asks in a box**: discard or go back, no typing and no pane. M. Meets
  walk-10 (quit after an export) and UX-16.
- **Full screen**, then full screen across several windows. M; several windows L (#146).
- **Workshop's own title bar, or none**: the window's space given to Workshop, only close,
  minimize and full screen kept, what the bar says now said as a notice; the top right's setup
  words moved down a row, below those buttons. M to L. Meets UX-06.

## 2. Text and input

- **One text box everywhere**, the standard Editor's included: a click shows it chosen with a
  blinking caret and selects its text, a setting turning the selecting off. M. Meets UX-17.
- **The Terminal**: Enter takes the completion the cursor has moved to, Shift+Enter enters early
  (S); an ask's round trip time shown (S).
- **Files' paths centre-truncated**: the drive, an ellipsis, as much of the end as fits; whole and
  scrolling while the pointer is over it. S to M. Meets #117 (a path cut inside a UTF-8 character,
  needs a decision).

## 3. The panes that hold data

- **Info, legible and easy to edit**: a row each for name, type and value, each editable; a typed
  row added. M. Meets UX-24 and UX-25.
- **Inventory as a game's**: a grid of slots in pixel-thick lines, growing as far as the screen
  goes; sorted by type with a row of icons or a dropdown; smaller text; an item's full schema in a
  tooltip, from one box Info keeps for that, and by a right-press Inspect. L. Meets UX-29 to UX-31.
- **An inventory item named by its schema by default**; a Shift-release asks for a name. S to M.
- **Loaded**: a search; a right-press menu with Reload, Inspect and Delete. M. Meets UX-18 and
  UX-19. The editor switch's messages are hard to find there today.
- **Compose**: docs, tags on messages, an organized catalog, the schema in readable form after the
  command's name and version. M. Meets UX-21 to UX-23.
- **Project**: it does not scroll (a defect, S); it wants a right-press menu, which overlaps
  Loaded's. Meets UX-32 and UX-33. Research first: Compose, Project and Loaded as one workflow, its
  examples tested end to end by the mouse alone. M for the research.
- **The Editor's right-press menu**: close, save, undo and redo with their keys shown, the basics.
  S to M. Meets UX-34 and UX-05.
- **The Hotkeys pane in accordions**, a section per pane, the desk's panes first, absent ones grey
  at the foot and still openable to their options. M.
- **Every right-press offers "Open <Schema> in Info"** for what it was pressed on. M.
- **A schema creator**, and **a test-data creator** filling a schema's fields, kept and reused
  through Inventory. L each.

## 4. Making things: the Builder, Flow, operators

- **The Builder made over**: visually intuitive, the important things first, only what a step
  needs; realize a button, by mouse and by keyboard. Its rules: the best component, then the
  smallest, then anything else; a component may be input and output at once; one is better than
  two, and turning two into one reusable is a good thing to leave the next maker. L. Meets UX-35
  and UX-12.
- **Flow made over, then a guided walkthrough** (decided; BL-WORK-32 in the backlog).
- **Two types that do not fit, dragged together, open a flow between them**: the visual type
  conversion pane, in its natural action. L.
- **Operator marks**: one button in a code tool marks existing code as holding operators, barely
  changing how it reads. L.
- **Algorithms and data structures**: the standard library's, and simple pure ones, possibly from
  well-tested open source. L and open-ended; what they serve (Flow's operators?) to be said.
- **A basic colour enum** (S; for what, to be said) and **images** (L).
- **Macros**: the official way inside them, with less ceremony for the weaver. M to L.
- **A build recipe for every pane**: Edit code says "no build recipe produces ..." for the panes
  without one. M. The Demo pane's development recipe was left out on purpose in the guides round
  (2026-10-25); this asks for every pane's.
- **A demo of Workshop improving itself**: a pane edited and reloaded in place, the change tasteful
  -- one pane pink, a font, a ripple at each press drawn by the graphics library alone -- since a
  real improvement would be one to commit. M, after the recipes.

## 5. Agents and Loom

- **Composed tools**: the agents' tool packages read for duplicated code, what they share drawn out
  into smaller tools. M, research first.
- **An expectation set before a send**: an agent says what it expects to hear before it sends, with
  a timeout that fails it, so what would otherwise be pushed to it (a notice's update) is caught by
  that expectation. M. Meets `workshop/act`'s expect steps and Loom's `ExpectRun`.
- **Authority limited to a session, and a permanent grant revoked**. M to L, Loom's.

## 6. Learning, reach and method

- **Challenges**, as LeetCode has them, in Loom: a pane that opens them, Start here as Challenge 1,
  a place for quests later. M.
- **A website for Zen**. L, outside the repositories.
- **An audit of workflows by the mouse alone**: how often the mouse cannot do it; how often a
  context is put aside for another, and whether the flow comes back to it; how far the mouse
  travels to the tools, split into the setup's share (from the nearest edge of the pane left) and
  the pane's own layout. M, research. Meets the hotkey audit (2026-10-23) and the Perspectives
  Lab's mouse rule.
